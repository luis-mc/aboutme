About

I've spent more than twenty years chasing one question: how do you build technology that genuinely moves a business forward?

That question took me from a Portuguese startup to Microsoft, then to co-founding Maincheck — acquired by FARFETCH in 2013 — and on to FARFETCH itself. I lead Technology as a single, connected discipline — Product, Engineering, Research, and Infrastructure working as one — behind a platform that brings the world's best luxury boutiques to customers in more than 190 countries.

I joined FARFETCH in 2013 and have grown with the company, from leading a small technology team to now CTO. Throughout this journey, I have focused on the key factors that determine a platform's success: solid architecture, prioritizing performance, scalability, security, and reliable data, and building a culture where everyone on the technology team can do their best work.

My interest in mobile technology began well before smartphones became common. I developed my skills at MobiComp, a Braga-based startup that Microsoft acquired in 2008, and I was part of the team that won the first Google Android Developer Challenge for the first Life360 app on Android. I studied Computer Science at the Universidade do Minho, and I still love learning, most recently about deep learning and machine learning engineering.

The Journey

FARFETCH 2013 — present

Chief Technology Officer

I lead Technology at FARFETCH end to end — Product, Engineering, Research, and Infrastructure under one roof. The mandate is simple to say and hard to do: set the platform's long-term direction, keep engineering excellent as it scales, and make sure technology is always serving the business and the customer.

  • Guided the architecture of the FARFETCH platform as it grew into one of the world's leading luxury marketplaces
  • Made AI and machine learning a strategic lever — for discovery, for the customer experience, and for how the business runs
  • Built and grew an international Technology organisation spanning multiple countries
  • Set the foundations for security, data governance, and infrastructure that operate reliably at global scale
FARFETCH earlier years

Head → SVP, Technology

I joined as FARFETCH was finding its feet and helped it sprint. I set the architectural direction during the company's steepest growth, and shaped the engineering principles and practices that let a young platform scale without losing its way.

Maincheck 2011 — 2015

Co-Founder

The company I started with others: a consultancy working exclusively on software quality management and testing. We ran independent application-lifecycle assessments, stood up test labs, and took on the work teams tend to postpone — performance, security, automation. I led internal research and product development, and worked as principal consultant on application lifecycle management. It came from a conviction I still hold, that quality isn't a phase bolted on at the end but part of how software gets built. FARFETCH bought the company in January 2013 — which is how I arrived there — and I stayed on its board until April 2015. Founding something put me on the other side of the table, a different education from any job.

Microsoft 2008 — 2011

Mobile Services R&D

When Microsoft acquired MobiComp in 2008, I joined to help stand up Microsoft's first mobile-services R&D centre in Europe, based in Braga. It was a front-row seat to the mobile revolution as it was happening — building cloud-connected services for a world that was only just going wireless.

Life360 early mobile

Android Engineering

I was part of the team behind the first Life360 application for Android — work that won a Google Android Developer Challenge, one of the platform's earliest global accolades. Building for Android when the SDK was brand new taught me how to ship on shifting ground.

MobiComp 2001 — 2008

Software Developer → Architect

Where it all began. I grew up as an engineer at MobiComp, a Braga startup, rising from developer to architect and helping drive product innovation across mobile operator data and video services, mobile banking platforms, and retail solutions — until Microsoft came knocking in 2008.
